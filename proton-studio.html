Station 02 / 05

Count what ionised

Imagine preparing 0.050 mol dm⁻³ of a monoprotic acid HA. Each time HA ionises, one A⁻ and one H₃O⁺ form. The grid shows only acid-derived particles: a hollow circle is HA; a solid square is A⁻. Hydronium and water are not drawn.

See the reasoning

0.02 × 0.050 = 0.0010 mol dm⁻³. The remaining 0.049 mol dm⁻³ is HA.

See the reasoning

0.0020 / 0.040 × 100 = 5%. The ionised acid is the part; the initial acid is the whole.

Discuss: How could a strong acid give the same hydronium concentration as the 2% sample?

Station 03 / 05

Turn the hydroxide dial

At 25 °C, [H₃O⁺] × [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ when concentrations are in mol dm⁻³. At neutrality the two concentrations are equal. Each step to the right on the dial multiplies [OH⁻] by ten. Predict what must happen to its partner, then try it.

pH = −log₁₀[H₃O⁺]. For example, [H₃O⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ gives pH 4.00. Use a calculator for concentrations between powers of ten; you can type 4e-12 for 4 × 10⁻¹².

See the reasoning

(1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴) / (2.5 × 10⁻³) = 4.0 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³.

See the reasoning

−log₁₀(4.0 × 10⁻¹²) = 11.40. The alkaline solution has less hydronium than pure water.
